Poppies, Pastures, Gold and Snow: Kyrgyzstan Through Four Seasons

2026/10/032 min readGo2Mountains

“When is the best time to come?” Our honest answer: it depends on what you want to see. Kyrgyzstan changes completely from season to season, and each one has something the others don’t.

Spring (March–May): red fields and a new year

Spring begins with a celebration. On 21 March, Kyrgyzstan celebrates Nooruz, the Persian New Year shared across Central Asia, with music, games, family feasts and sumolok, a sweet wheat dish cooked through the night.

By mid to late May, the foothills and valleys burst into poppy fields, red waves of flowers against green hills and snowy peaks. Days are mild; mornings and evenings are still cool.

Good for: photography, gentle hikes, flowers, and travellers who prefer cool weather and few crowds. High passes may still be snowed in.

Summer (June–August): the nomad season

This is when the mountains open up. Herding families move their yurts and animals to the high pastures, and places like Song-Kul (3,016 m) can only be visited now. Issyk-Kul is warm enough for a swim, and the hiking and riding seasons are in full swing.

Good for: yurt stays, horse treks, nomad culture, lakes and mountains. Bring layers: nights at altitude are cold even in August.

Autumn (September–October): the golden season

Many travellers think this is Kyrgyzstan at its most beautiful. Valleys turn gold and orange, skies are clear and the light is soft all day. Markets are full of apples, pears, grapes and walnuts. In September, families bring their herds back down from the pastures.

Good for: photography, slow travel, valley hikes and riding through golden forests. Song-Kul usually closes for the season by late September.

Winter (November–February): quiet and snow

Winter is cold and quiet, and the mountains turn white. Karakol is the country’s main ski base, and hot springs feel even better with snow around them. Issyk-Kul doesn’t freeze, so the lake shore stays open all year.

Good for: skiing, hot springs, snowy landscapes and seeing a side of Kyrgyzstan few visitors know.

So, when should you come?

  • First visit, want everything: late June to early September.
  • Flowers and photography: mid to late May.
  • Golden colours and fewer people: late September to October.
  • Snow and something different: January to February.

Whichever season you choose, tell us your dates and we’ll design the route around what the mountains are doing at that time of year.
