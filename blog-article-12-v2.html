Riding to Song-Kol: 8 days in the highlands

2024/02/182 min readBek & Ruby

Song-Kul is where we return every season to reset. No billboards, no rush—just grazing herds, yurt smoke, and a sky that goes on forever. Our latest 8-day loop brought two families and a filmmaker friend. Here’s what stood out.

Why Song-Kul still feels wild

At 3,016 m, Song-Kul is only reachable in summer, when herding families move their animals up to the high pastures. That short season is exactly what keeps it special: for a few months, the shore becomes a scattered village of yurts, and then it goes quiet again.

1. The pace matters

We slowed the drive from Bishkek so the kids could stretch, taste fresh kymyz, and meet eagle hunters on the south shore of Issyk-Kul. Long drives feel shorter when they’re broken up by real stops.

2. Yurt nights stay cozy

At 3,000 m the air cools fast after sunset. We book yurts with good stoves and extra felt layers. Our tip: ask for the north-facing yurts—they’re quieter when tour buses roll in late.

3. Horses at your pace

We match horses to riders. The kids rode on a lead rope; confident riders took a ridge-line loop. No forced gallops—just the right amount of steppe wind.

4. Food is half the memory

Our host Aigul insisted on teaching everyone to pull lagman noodles. Dinners were family style: plov, salads, and endless tea.

Vegetarian? Tell us early. We plan extra vegetables and dairy with the camps before you arrive.

5. Bring layers, not luggage

Even in August, the nights bite. A down jacket, wool socks, and a neck buff make the difference between “cold” and “cozy.” We keep a spare blanket set in the car, just in case.

6. The best light of the trip

Sunrise hits a mirror-still lake. Photographers: 5:30–6:30 am is gold—the mountain ridge glows before the wind picks up.

7. Respect the rhythm

We follow our hosts’ lead. When the family moves the herds, we step back and watch. Kids help with water buckets; adults learn to tie the yurt door properly.

8. Leave lighter footprints

We pack out our trash, avoid single-use plastics, and keep groups small. The fewer the cars, the more you hear: hooves, stove crackles, and quiet voices in the dark.

Song-Kul changes with every season. If you’d like this route at your own pace—slower for families, faster for trekkers—get in touch, and we’ll tailor the loop so it feels like your trip, not just our plan.
