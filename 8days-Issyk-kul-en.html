The Kyrgyzstan we want to share with you

Issyk-Kul Loop × Autumn Valleys

This trip is designed for travelers who love a slow pace, rest, nature and nomadic culture. It takes you deep into Kyrgyzstan's most unspoiled and moving valleys and lakes. Alongside the classic highlights, we add experiences arranged only by Bek & Ruby, so the journey has more warmth and depth.

We travel in small groups and run only a few departures each year. We care about quality and depth of experience, and make sure every traveler is well looked after.

It's especially well suited to families with children or older travelers. Daily driving times are moderate, leaving plenty of time to enjoy the scenery, photograph the morning and evening light, or simply slow down and rest in nature.

Issyk-Kul vs. Song-Kul: how are the two lakes different?

This trip focuses on Issyk-Kul and does not go to Song-Kul. The comparison below is only to help you choose the trip that suits you.

Issyk-Kul Lake This trip

  • About 1,600 m above sea level
  • Towns, hotels and cafés by the lake, with more comforts
  • Good for families, older travelers and first visits to Kyrgyzstan
  • Lakeside walks, canyons, hot springs and cultural experiences
  • A relaxed, comfortable trip

Song-Kul Lake Not on this trip

  • About 3,000 m above sea level
  • High pastures and yurts: real nomadic life
  • Reached by a long drive or on horseback; more remote
  • Good for travelers who love nature, riding and adventure
  • Needs more stamina and adjustment to altitude

Spring (March–May)

Snow starts melting on the peaks, the grasslands turn green, and wildflowers and poppy fields bloom in the valleys (mid-May). Days are cool and mornings and evenings still cold, so bring warm layers. Good for easy hikes, flowers and photographing sunrise and sunset.

Summer (June–August)

Valleys and lakes are at their greenest, Issyk-Kul is warm, and nomad families move up to the high pastures. Days are warm and nights a little cool. Good for riding, hiking, relaxing by the lake and nomad culture.

Autumn (September–November)

The high pastures turn gold and orange, the morning and evening light is beautiful, and the lake reflects the autumn colors. Cool, dry and quieter: the best time for photos and slow travel.

  2. Day1

    Bishkek → Osh Bazaar → Chunkurchak

    From the city into an autumn valley

    BazaarHorse riding

    Start with everyday life in the capital, head into a mountain valley and ride across the open grassland: your Central Asian journey begins.

    After breakfast, your guide walks you through the map of the whole 8-day route and today's plan.

    In the morning we visit the lively Osh Bazaar: spices, dried fruit, the meat hall and bakeries pulling fresh nan from clay ovens, real Kyrgyz city life among the crowds.

    Lunch is at a restaurant built in traditional Kyrgyz style, with yurt-inspired architecture and authentic dishes.

    In the afternoon we drive about an hour to Chunkurchak Valley and switch to horses for a ride of about 2 hours deep into the valley. In autumn the slopes and meadows turn gold and brown, and the rhythm of the city stays behind.

    In the evening we check in to a mountain resort in the valley for dinner and a good rest on the first night of the trip.

  3. Day2

    Chunkurchak → Burana Tower → Chon Kemin

    From a Silk Road tower to valley life

    Silk Road towerModern yurt stay

    Morning views in the mountains, a Silk Road tower, and a quiet evening in Chon Kemin.

    Early risers start with photos at a viewpoint and a short hike above the resort, followed by breakfast.

    We then drive to Burana Tower. Standing alone on the steppe, it was once part of the Silk Road city of Balasagun. Climb the tower for views over the plain to the distant Tien Shan, and hear the story of the Silk Road civilization that once flourished here.

    Lunch is in a local family's home near Burana, where the hosts cook a home-style meal for us.

    In the afternoon we continue to Chon Kemin Valley. We check in to a modern yurt stay with beautiful mountain views, with time to walk through the meadows and enjoy the snowy peaks before dinner.

    • DrivingAbout 130 km, about 4 hours
    • MorningViewpoint photos and a short hike
    • MealsBreakfast, home-cooked lunch at a local family's home near Burana, dinner

  4. Day3

    Chon Kemin → Bokonbaevo

    Chon Kemin hike & the eagle hunter

    Mountain hikeEagle hunting

    We start the day in the mountains. After breakfast, we set out on a morning hike in Chon Kemin, with time to walk slowly through the valley's meadows and forests, breathe the fresh mountain air and enjoy the views before we leave.

    Lunch is home-cooked at our stay in Chon Kemin. Afterwards we drive to Bokonbaevo on the south shore of Issyk-Kul. The scenery changes from the northern valleys and steppe to the wider lake and mountain views around Issyk-Kul, and we stop every two to three hours, so the drive stays comfortable.

    In the afternoon we meet a local eagle hunter for a traditional eagle-hunting demonstration. With the hunter's introduction and demonstration, you get close to this long-standing nomadic tradition. For a hunter, a golden eagle isn't just a hunting tool. It's a partner that takes years of time, training and trust, and a way of life passed down through generations.

    Dinner is home-cooked at a local guesthouse, and then we check in to Altyn Oimok or Elestet in Bokonbaevo, to rest by the south shore and get a feel for village life far from the city.

  5. Day4

    Bokonbaevo → Skazka Canyon → Barskoon → Jeti-Oguz → Karakol

    A day in nature

    CanyonsWaterfall hike

    Colorful canyons, a forest waterfall and red-rock cliffs: the day with the most varied landscapes of the trip.

    After an early breakfast we drive to Skazka, the Fairy Tale Canyon. Layers of orange-red, ochre and light-brown rock have been shaped by wind and rain into castles, ridges and fantasy creatures, with the deep blue lake behind them.

    Lunch is home-cooked at a local guesthouse.

    In Barskoon Valley we hike about 2 hours along a pine-forest trail to Barskoon Waterfall. You can stop at the first waterfall or continue up to the second.

    Late in the afternoon we reach the red-rock canyon of Jeti-Oguz. The car takes us up, and we walk about 15 minutes back down through the canyon, past the famous "Seven Bulls" rocks, while hearing the local legends of the valley.

    In the evening we arrive in Karakol for dinner at a restaurant with refined Central Asian dishes, then check in to our hotel.

    • DrivingBokonbaevo → Skazka Canyon: 30 km, 40 minutes

    Karakol → Karakol Gorge → Karakol

    Karakol Gorge & a day of many cultures

    Gorge hikeMany cultures

    A hike in Karakol Gorge, a picnic barbecue, and the many cultures of Karakol.

    After breakfast we drive into Karakol Gorge for a hike of about 2.5 hours through spruce forest beside the mountain river.

    Lunch is a picnic in the gorge, the Kyrgyz people's favorite way to relax outdoors. Depending on conditions, it's a barbecue picnic or a simple picnic.

    In the afternoon we visit Karakol's two remarkable buildings: the wooden Holy Trinity Cathedral, built by Russian settlers, and the Dungan Mosque, built without a single nail in a blend of Chinese and Islamic styles. Then we browse local crafts at the One Village One Product shop and stop at the market for snacks for tomorrow.

    Dinner is Dungan food: tangy, slightly spicy cold noodles (ashlan-fu) and pirozhki.

    Karakol → Ak-Suu hot spring → Altyn-Arashan

    Ak-Suu hot spring & a hidden Tien Shan valley

    Hot springs4WD

    A morning soak in a hot spring, then up a rough mountain track to a hidden valley below snowy peaks.

    After breakfast we relax at the Ak-Suu hot spring for about two hours.

    Then we climb by 4x4 (for groups of four or more, a Soviet military truck) on a rough 30 km track to Altyn-Arashan, an alpine valley at about 2,500 m, where wooden cabins and yurts are scattered between meadows and snowy peaks.

    After lunch at the guesthouse, the afternoon is free to relax or take an easy walk in the valley. Dinner is at the guesthouse, and at night the sky is clear enough for stargazing.

    Altyn-Arashan → Karakol → Cholpon-Ata

    From the mountains back to the lake

    Hike downLakeside

    Walk down from the mountains, then back to the shore of Issyk-Kul.

    After breakfast, you can say goodbye to Altyn-Arashan on foot: a walk of about 2.5 hours down the valley to a viewpoint halfway, where the car picks you up. You can also ride all the way.

    We drive down to Aksu village and have lunch at a fish restaurant.

    Then we follow the north shore of Issyk-Kul for about three hours to Cholpon-Ata. After checking in, we walk through the park to the lakeshore, and dinner is at a local Kyrgyz halal restaurant, a favorite of the locals (no alcohol is served).

    Cholpon-Ata → Balykchy → Bishkek

    A boat on the lake, a petroglyph museum, and back to the city

    Boat tripPetroglyphs

    A boat on the lake, ancient petroglyphs, and back to the city.

    After breakfast we go out on Issyk-Kul by boat for about an hour, with the snowy peaks of the north shore stretching above the water.

    Back on shore, we visit the Cholpon-Ata open-air petroglyph site, where rock carvings from the Bronze Age through the nomadic era are preserved. If you like, there's also time for the local history museum.

    We drive to Balykchy for lunch, then on to Bishkek, arriving in the early evening for a farewell dinner at a restaurant.

    After the tour, we take you back to your hotel in the capital to rest (or, if you have an evening flight, we take you to the airport for free).

Food

Authentic Kyrgyz dishes and fresh seasonal fruit

On the trip, you may taste these classic dishes:

Beshbarmak

Hand-cut noodles with boiled meat. The most iconic Kyrgyz dish, served for celebrations and to honor guests.

Kuurdak

Fried lamb or beef with potatoes and onions: everyday nomad home cooking.

Plov

Rice slow-cooked with carrots and meat, rich and fragrant.

Lagman

Hand-pulled noodles with vegetables and beef, a favorite across Central Asia.

Samsa

Meat pastries baked in a traditional clay oven, crisp outside and juicy inside.

Shashlik

Skewers grilled over charcoal in the open air: the smell you'll meet most often on the road.

Snacks and drinks

  • Boorsok: fried dough pieces that are a must at every nomad feast; with jam and hot tea, they're the locals' favorite afternoon snack.
  • Maksym / Chalap / Jarma: traditional fermented grain drinks, refreshing and slightly sour, loved by locals every day.
  • Kymyz: fermented mare's milk, the classic nomad drink, slightly sour and good for digestion.

Seasonal fruit

  • Summer: cherries (June), apricots (July, famous across Central Asia), melons, and all kinds of berries (raspberries, strawberries, blackcurrants, blueberries).
  • Autumn: apples from around Issyk-Kul, juicy pears, and the sweetest grapes in September.
