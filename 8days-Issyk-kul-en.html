Issyk-Kul Loop × Autumn Valleys

This trip is designed for travelers who love a slow pace, rest, nature and nomadic culture. It takes you deep into Kyrgyzstan's most unspoiled and moving valleys and lakes. Alongside the classic highlights, we add experiences arranged only by Bek & Ruby, so the journey has more warmth and depth.

We travel in small groups and run only a few departures each year. We care about quality and depth of experience, and make sure every traveler is well looked after.

It's especially well suited to families with children or older travelers. Daily driving times are moderate, leaving plenty of time to enjoy the scenery, photograph the morning and evening light, or simply slow down and rest in nature.

Issyk-Kul vs. Song-Kul: how are the two lakes different?

This trip focuses on Issyk-Kul and does not go to Song-Kul. The comparison below is only to help you choose the trip that suits you.

Issyk-Kul Lake This trip

  • About 1,600 m above sea level
  • Towns, hotels and cafés by the lake, with more comforts
  • Good for families, older travelers and first visits to Kyrgyzstan
  • Lakeside walks, canyons, hot springs and cultural experiences
  • A relaxed, comfortable trip

Song-Kul Lake Not on this trip

  • About 3,000 m above sea level
  • High pastures and yurts: real nomadic life
  • Reached by a long drive or on horseback; more remote
  • Good for travelers who love nature, riding and adventure
  • Needs more stamina and adjustment to altitude

Spring (March–May)

Snow starts melting on the peaks, the grasslands turn green, and wildflowers and poppy fields bloom in the valleys (mid-May). Days are cool and mornings and evenings still cold, so bring warm layers. Good for easy hikes, flowers and photographing sunrise and sunset.

Summer (June–August)

Valleys and lakes are at their greenest, Issyk-Kul is warm, and nomad families move up to the high pastures. Days are warm and nights a little cool. Good for riding, hiking, relaxing by the lake and nomad culture.

Autumn (September–November)

The high pastures turn gold and orange, the morning and evening light is beautiful, and the lake reflects the autumn colors. Cool, dry and quieter: the best time for photos and slow travel.

  1. Day1

    Bishkek → Osh Bazaar → Chunkurchak

    From the city into an autumn valley

    BazaarHorse riding

    Start with everyday life in the capital, head into a mountain valley and walk across the open grassland: your Central Asian journey begins.

    The journey starts in Bishkek. In the morning we walk through the lively Osh Bazaar, past stalls piled high with spices, dried fruit, fresh produce, fabrics and household goods, feeling real Kyrgyz city life among the crowds.

    Lunch is at the Supara Ethno Complex. Inspired by traditional Kyrgyz architecture and nomadic culture, it's a place to taste authentic dishes in a setting full of local character, and to start getting to know this land through its food.

    In the afternoon we leave the city and drive about an hour to Chunkurchak Valley. There we switch to horses for a ride of about 2 hours deep into the valley. In autumn Chunkurchak has completely different colors from summer: the slopes and meadows slowly turn gold and brown. Riding slowly along the trail, in the wide valley and cool air, we leave the rhythm of the city behind.

    In the evening we check in to the Supara Chunkurchak mountain resort. Surrounded by peaks, it's quiet and comfortable: a dinner in the mountains, a good rest, and a warm, easy first night of the trip.

    • DrivingBishkek → Osh Bazaar: about 10–15 km, 40 minutes
      Osh Bazaar → Chunkurchak: 45 km, about 1–1.5 hours
    • Horse ridingAbout 2 hours
    • LunchKyrgyz dishes at the Supara Ethno Complex
    • OvernightSupara Chunkurchak mountain resort: great for photos and for relaxing
  2. Day2

    Chunkurchak → Burana Tower → Chon Kemin

    From a Silk Road tower to valley life

    Silk Road towerVillage homestay

    After breakfast we leave Chunkurchak and head east, gradually leaving the city and main roads behind for the valleys and villages of Kyrgyzstan.

    On the way we stop at Burana Tower. Standing alone on the steppe, it was once part of the Silk Road city of Balasagun. Climb the tower for views over the wide plain to the distant Tien Shan, and learn about the Silk Road civilization that once flourished in Central Asia.

    In the afternoon we reach Chon Kemin Valley. There aren't many planned "sights" here; what's special is the slow, real life of the valley. Walk through the village and meadows with snowy peaks on the horizon, sit down for tea, watch cows and sheep wander past, and feel the quiet of the valley.

    Tonight we stay at a family homestay in the valley, where the hosts cook all the meals, many with ingredients from their own garden or nearby farms. Rather than rushing to the next sight, today is about giving time to Chon Kemin: walking slowly, looking closely, and feeling the most natural side of Kyrgyz village life.

    • DrivingAbout 130 km, about 4 hours
    • MealsAll meals cooked by the homestay hosts
    • OvernightKok Archa guest house or Etno Kompleks Ala Kush

  3. Day3

    Chon Kemin → Bokonbaevo

    To the south shore of Issyk-Kul: eagle-hunting culture

    Eagle huntingFelt making

    After breakfast we say goodbye to Chon Kemin and drive to Bokonbaevo on the south shore of Issyk-Kul. The drive takes about five hours, and the scenery changes from the northern valleys and steppe to the wider lake and mountain views around Issyk-Kul. We stop every two to three hours, so the long drive stays comfortable.

    Around midday we arrive at an eagle hunter's home for a traditional Kyrgyz home-cooked lunch. Afterwards comes the eagle-hunting experience: with the hunter's introduction and demonstration, you get close to this long-standing nomadic tradition. For a hunter, a golden eagle isn't just a hunting tool. It's a partner that takes years of time, training and trust, and a way of life passed down through generations.

    In the afternoon we try traditional Kyrgyz felt-carpet making, from natural wool and pattern layout to the felting process, taking part in an ancient craft born from nomadic life. Many of the colorful carpets you see in yurts and homes are made through steps like these, all by hand.

    In the evening we check in to Elestet, a modern yurt stay in Bokonbaevo, slow down in this small town on the south shore, enjoy dinner and rest, and get a feel for village life far from the city.

    • Driving270 km, 3.5–4.5 hours
    • LunchTraditional Kyrgyz home cooking at the eagle hunter's home
    • OvernightElestet, a modern yurt stay

  4. Day4

    Bokonbaevo → Skazka Canyon → Barskoon → Jeti-Oguz → Karakol

    A day in nature

    CanyonsWaterfall hike

    Today we travel east along the south shore of Issyk-Kul: the day with the most varied landscapes of the whole trip.

    First comes Skazka, the Fairy Tale Canyon. Layers of orange-red, ochre and light-brown rock, worn by wind and rain, have formed shapes like castles, ridges and fantasy creatures. Walk through the canyon and climb higher for a view of the deep blue lake: desert-like red rocks against the water make a striking contrast.

    Next is Barskoon Valley, where the scenery changes from dry, colorful canyons to forests and mountain streams. A 1–2 hour hike along a pine trail leads to Barskoon Waterfall. Depending on your energy, you can stop at the first waterfall or continue up to the second. With forest, streams and valley views all the way, it's the most pleasant hike of the day.

    In the afternoon we continue to the red-rock canyon of Jeti-Oguz to see the famous "Seven Bulls" rocks up close, and hear the local stories and legends of this valley.

    In the evening we reach Karakol. Dinner is at Dastorkon restaurant, with refined Central Asian and Kyrgyz dishes. From colorful canyons in the morning to a forest waterfall at midday and red-rock valleys in the evening, we pass through completely different landscapes in one day, and end it with a good dinner in Karakol.

    • DrivingBokonbaevo → Skazka Canyon: 30 km, 40 minutes
      Skazka Canyon → Barskoon Waterfall: 70 km, about 1.5 hours
      Barskoon Waterfall → Barskoon village: 10 km, 15–20 minutes
    • HikingBarskoon Waterfall, about 1–2 hours
    • DinnerDastorkon restaurant
    • OvernightMatsunoki Japanese guesthouse or 78 Hotel

    Good to know

    On the waterfall hike, you can stop at the first waterfall or continue up to the second, depending on how you feel.

  5. Day5

    Karakol City → Karakol Gorge

    Karakol town and a valley picnic

    Many culturesPicnic

    A town of many cultures, spruce forest and a valley picnic: a real day in Karakol.

    Karakol is the most important town in eastern Kyrgyzstan, and the place where its many cultures meet most clearly. In the morning we visit two remarkable buildings: the wooden Holy Trinity Orthodox Cathedral, built in 1895, a reminder of the Russian settlers who made their home here; and the Dungan Mosque, built by Central Asia's Dungan people without a single nail, in a rare blend of Chinese and Islamic styles.

    After browsing the souvenir shops, lunch is Dungan street food: tangy, slightly spicy cold noodles (ashlan-fu) and pirozhki, crisp outside and soft inside, everyday flavors for locals. Then we visit the market to buy everything we want for a picnic, and head to Karakol Gorge for a hike: the hidden spot from Bek & Ruby's videos. A picnic outdoors is the Kyrgyz people's favorite way to relax, and today we join them.

    • Driving25 km, 40 minutes
    • LunchDungan snacks: cold noodles and pirozhki
    • OvernightMatsunoki Japanese guesthouse or 78 Hotel
    • VideoWatch the video

    Good to know

    We originally planned an autumn hike on the Aksu trail, but in autumn parts of it are overgrown with thorny plants, which makes walking uncomfortable, so we moved the hike to Karakol Gorge. Karakol Gorge is just as beautiful in autumn: a wide valley with alpine forest, streams and views of the Tien Shan, on a comfortable trail.

  6. Day6

    Karakol → Altyn-Arashan

    A hidden Tien Shan valley: the golden hot springs

    Hot springs4WD

    Ride a 4WD into a hidden Tien Shan valley and let body and mind fully relax among snowy peaks.

    After breakfast we leave Karakol by 4x4 (for groups of four or more, a Soviet military truck) for Altyn-Arashan. The 30 km road takes about two hours. It's bumpy, but the valley views make it hard to close your eyes.

    Altyn-Arashan means "golden spa". At about 2,500 m, its wooden cabins are scattered between meadows and snowy peaks. On arrival, change into a swimsuit and slip into the natural hot springs, with hot water rising from the ground and snowy mountains in view: many guests say it was one of the most unforgettable moments of the trip. Meals are provided by the guesthouse, and the night sky is incredibly clear.

    • DrivingKarakol → Altyn-Arashan: 30 km, about 2 hours (off-road: very bumpy but a classic)
    • AltitudeAbout 2,500 m
    • MealsLunch and dinner at the guesthouse
    • OvernightAla-Ku Guesthouse, in a yurt
    • VideoWatch the video

    Good to know

    The off-road track is very bumpy; groups of four or more travel in a Soviet military truck. Remember to bring a swimsuit for the hot springs.

  7. Day7

    Altyn-Arashan → Karakol → Cholpon-Ata

    From the mountains back to the lake

    Hike downLakeside

    You wake up in the Altyn-Arashan valley, and today you can say goodbye to it on foot. The walk down the valley takes about 2.5 hours, beside snowy peaks, forest and rushing streams. Halfway down, the 4WD picks you up and takes you back to the village at the foot of the mountains.

    Lunch is near Karakol, with the area's famous trout, to recharge after the mountains. Then we drive west along the north shore of Issyk-Kul for about three hours to Cholpon-Ata, the most holiday-like town on the north shore.

    In the evening, walk along the lakeshore and look out over the wide lake and distant mountains, slowing down as the trip nears its end. Dinner is at Mubarak, an Arabic restaurant, and afterwards you can get desserts and ice cream at Kulikov next door. Back from the mountain hike to a lakeside town, there's no rush tonight: just enjoy the relaxed holiday feel of the north shore.

    • DrivingAltyn-Arashan → Karakol: 30 km, about 2–3 hours (off-road)
      Karakol → Cholpon-Ata: 140–150 km, about 3 hours
    • HikingWalk down, about 2.5 hours (optional)
    • MealsTrout for lunch, dinner at Mubarak Arabic restaurant
    • OvernightOlimp hotel or Zaton guesthouse

    Good to know

    The hike down is optional; you can also ride the 4WD all the way.

  8. Day8

    Cholpon-Ata → Bishkek

    A boat on the lake, a petroglyph museum, and back to the city

    Boat tripPetroglyphs

    The last day. The highlight this morning is a private boat trip on the lake: gliding across open water with the snowy peaks of the north shore stretching above the lake in a wide, magnificent panorama. Take photos, soak up the sun, or simply sit back on the boat.

    Back on shore, we visit the Cholpon-Ata open-air petroglyph museum, where rock carvings from the Bronze Age, about 4,000 years ago, through the nomadic era are preserved. On the way back we stop at a petrol station for coffee and a break, easing slowly from the rhythm of the mountains back to city life. In the evening we arrive in Bishkek for dinner at a restaurant.

    • DrivingCholpon-Ata → Bishkek: 270 km, about 4 hours
    • DinnerRestaurant in Bishkek

Food

Authentic Kyrgyz dishes and fresh seasonal fruit

On the trip, you may taste these classic dishes:

Beshbarmak

Hand-cut noodles with boiled meat. The most iconic Kyrgyz dish, served for celebrations and to honor guests.

Kuurdak

Fried lamb or beef with potatoes and onions: everyday nomad home cooking.

Plov

Rice slow-cooked with carrots and meat, rich and fragrant.

Lagman

Hand-pulled noodles with vegetables and beef, a favorite across Central Asia.

Samsa

Meat pastries baked in a traditional clay oven, crisp outside and juicy inside.

Shashlik

Skewers grilled over charcoal in the open air: the smell you'll meet most often on the road.

Snacks and drinks

  • Boorsok: fried dough pieces that are a must at every nomad feast; with jam and hot tea, they're the locals' favorite afternoon snack.
  • Maksym / Chalap / Jarma: traditional fermented grain drinks, refreshing and slightly sour, loved by locals every day.
  • Kymyz: fermented mare's milk, the classic nomad drink, slightly sour and good for digestion.

Seasonal fruit

  • Summer: cherries (June), apricots (July, famous across Central Asia), melons, and all kinds of berries (raspberries, strawberries, blackcurrants, blueberries).
  • Autumn: apples from around Issyk-Kul, juicy pears, and the sweetest grapes in September.
