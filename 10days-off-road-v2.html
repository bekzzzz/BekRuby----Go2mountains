The journey

A true outdoor adventure in the Tien Shan

You'll ride for two days over a 3,400 m pass to Son-Kol Lake, spend a slow day with a nomad family, and travel deep into the remote border highlands to Kel-Suu, a lake surrounded by sheer canyon walls. Along the way, you'll stay in yurt camps and guesthouses, share home-cooked meals, meet a golden eagle hunter, and learn how a yurt is built.

Expect real mountain conditions: horse riding, off-road driving, high altitude, cold nights, and some days without showers or phone signal. In return, you'll see a side of Kyrgyzstan that few travelers reach.

Before you book

Please read this first

This is not an easy tour, but if it suits you, it will be one of the most rewarding trips of your life. Each day is different, and knowing what to expect makes the journey much more enjoyable.

Every traveler should read this personally, not just the person who organizes the trip for friends or family. Please also read the full day-by-day itinerary before booking.

Where you'll stay

4 nights in yurt camps (Kilemche, Son-Kol north and south, and Elestet, a modern yurt stay in Bokonbaevo), sometimes a glamping tent. 2 nights in a simple wooden cottage in Kok-Kiya. 3 nights in guesthouses and hotels. Some camps are quiet; others can be busy with other travelers.

Showers

There's no shower for 2 days in a row during the horse trek (Days 2–3). Some mountain camps have only a basic shared shower. You'll have hot showers in guesthouses and hotels before and after these sections.

Toilets

In mountain camps, toilets are simple and shared with other guests, often outdoors. In some guesthouses, bathrooms may also be shared.

Wi-Fi, signal & electricity

No Wi-Fi or phone signal on Day 2. Elsewhere Wi-Fi and signal are often weak or slow, so don't count on video calls or uploading photos. Charging is limited in the mountains, so please bring power banks.

Food

All meals are included. In the mountains there's no menu: we eat what the yurt camps and families prepare, simple home-style Kyrgyz food. Tell us about dietary needs (vegetarian, no lamb, allergies) when you book.

Weather

Sunshine, rain, wind, or even snow on high passes, also in summer. Days can be warm and nights very cold above 3,000 m. Your guide may change or replace activities for your safety.

Long drives & off-road

Some days include 4–5.5 hours of driving, partly on bumpy off-road mountain roads. Tiring, but they take you to places few travelers reach.

Altitude

Much of the trip is between 2,500 m and 3,500 m. Most people adapt well, but some feel headaches or tiredness. Tell us about any heart, lung, or other health conditions before booking.

Horse riding

No experience needed, but be comfortable with about 4 hours a day in the saddle on Days 2 and 3, plus about 4 hours round trip to Kel-Suu on Day 7. Local horse guides ride with you, and we match each horse to each rider.

Travel insurance

Insurance covering horse riding, high altitude, and emergency evacuation is required.

Border zone

Kel-Suu is in a border zone, so we need your passport details in advance to arrange your permit. Please send them by the deadline we give you.

Why it's worth it

Here, the pace of life slows down

No traffic jams, no notifications, no rush. Just wide open valleys, fresh mountain air, and the sound of wind across the grass. Riding through quiet pastures, sitting by a still mountain lake, drinking tea in a yurt as the evening light fades over the peaks. At night, the silence is so deep you can hear the horses grazing outside.

Many travelers say it's the first time in years they've truly switched off.

If you're looking for a break from noise, screens, and schedules, the mountains of Kyrgyzstan are waiting.

Day by day

The itinerary

  1. Day1

    Bishkek → Burana → Kyzart

    Silk Road tower & first mountain views

    Silk RoadDrive

    Your journey begins with a drive east from Bishkek to Burana Tower, an 11th-century minaret standing alone in the Chuy Valley, surrounded by ancient stone figures called balbals. Your guide shares the story of the Silk Road city that once stood here. Lunch is a local favorite: kattama, a flaky layered bread baked fresh, served with soup and tea at a roadside family café. In the afternoon, the road climbs into the Tien Shan mountains, with a stop at Kyz-Art Pass for the first wide mountain views. You arrive in Kyzart, the traditional gateway to Son-Kol, in time for a short village walk and a home-cooked dinner. Over dinner, your guide prepares you for the horse trek ahead.

    • DrivingAbout 5.5 hours, with stops
    • Highest pointKyz-Art Pass
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, dinner
    • OvernightVillage guesthouse, Kyzart
    • ShowerYes, in a separate building outside the guesthouse
    • Wi-FiYes
  2. Day2

    Kyzart → Kilemche Valley

    Into the saddle

    Horse trek · day 1

    After breakfast, you meet your horse and the local horse guides who will ride with you for the next two days. Before setting off, your guide gives a careful safety briefing and helps you get comfortable in the saddle. Then the trek begins: about four hours across rolling summer pastures, with regular breaks to rest and enjoy the views. You arrive in the wide Kilemche Valley, where herders bring their animals for the summer, in time for lunch at a small yurt camp. The afternoon is yours to rest, walk in the valley, or simply enjoy the quiet. After a sunset walk, dinner is served in the yurt, followed by a night under a sky full of stars.

    • Horse ridingAbout 4 hours, with breaks
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, dinner
    • OvernightYurt camp, Kilemche Valley
    • ShowerNo
    • ToiletSimple outdoor toilet, shared
    • Wi-FiNo Wi-Fi or phone signal

    Good to know

    Your main luggage travels separately by car and meets you at Son-Kol on Day 3. Please pack a small overnight bag for tonight with warm clothes, toiletries, and a power bank. Helmets and riding gloves are provided.

  3. Day3

    Kilemche → Jalgyz-Karagai Pass → Son-Kol

    Over the pass to Son-Kol

    Horse trek · day 23,400 m

    Today is the highlight of the trek. You set off early, while the mountain air is still and the light is soft, and ride slowly up to Jalgyz-Karagai Pass at 3,400 m. From the top, the whole high plateau opens up below you, and you catch your first sight of Son-Kol Lake shining in the distance. After time for photos and a snack, you descend through wide meadows to your yurt camp on the lake's northern shore, where your luggage is waiting. Lunch is served at the camp, and the afternoon is free to rest, walk along the shore, or play board games with your group. As evening falls, the lake turns golden, and after dinner the night sky at 3,000 m is unforgettable.

    • Horse ridingAbout 4 hours, with breaks
    • Highest pointJalgyz-Karagai Pass, 3,400 m
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, dinner
    • OvernightYurt camp or glamping tent, Son-Kol northern shore (3,016 m)
    • ShowerNo
    • ToiletSimple outdoor toilet, shared
    • Wi-FiWeak Wi-Fi at the camp, no phone signal

    Good to know

    It's cold and windy on the pass, even in summer, so keep a warm jacket, gloves, and a rain jacket in your day bag. On the steepest parts of the descent, you can walk beside your horse. If there's snow or a storm on the pass, your guide may decide to take you to Son-Kol by car instead, for your safety.

  4. Day4

    Son-Kol north → nomad family → Son-Kol south

    A slow day in the highlands

    Nomad family

    There's no rush today. If you're an early riser, watch the sunrise over the lake before breakfast. Then you drive across the open grasslands to visit a nomad family who spends the summer on the shores of Son-Kol. You're welcomed into their yurt for lunch and spend the afternoon learning about their way of life: how they care for their horses, make kymyz (fermented mare's milk) and fresh dairy, and live through the summer at 3,000 m. Later, the drive continues along the lake to your yurt camp on the southern shore, with stops for photos along the way. The evening is free for a sunset walk before dinner.

    • DrivingAbout 3 hours in total, with stops
    • Altitude3,016 m
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch with a nomad family, dinner
    • OvernightYurt camp, Son-Kol southern shore
    • ShowerShared shower at the camp
    • ToiletSimple outdoor toilet, shared

    Good to know

    Activities with the family depend on the season and their daily work, so each visit is a little different. Please remove your shoes when entering the yurt, and ask before taking photos of the family.

  5. Day5

    Son-Kol → Moldo-Ashuu Pass → Naryn

    Down the "32 Parrots" road

    Rest & recovery

    After a final morning by the lake, you leave Son-Kol by the Moldo-Ashuu Pass road. On the way down, you stop above the famous "32 Parrots" switchbacks, a road that zigzags dramatically down the mountainside. The scenery changes as you descend toward Naryn, a mountain town on the Naryn River. You arrive in time for lunch, then check in to a comfortable hotel, where a hot shower and a proper bed are waiting. The afternoon is completely free: rest, do some laundry, visit a café, or walk along the river. Dinner is at the hotel, where your guide also prepares you for the next two days in the remote Kel-Suu area.

    • DrivingAbout 4 hours, with stops
    • Highest pointMoldo-Ashuu Pass
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, dinner
    • OvernightHotel in Naryn, private bathroom
    • ShowerYes, hot shower in your room
    • Wi-FiYes

    Good to know

    Naryn is your last chance for an ATM, a pharmacy, and full phone charging before the Kel-Suu area. Many travelers use the free afternoon for laundry.

  6. Day6

    Naryn → Bosogo gorge → Kyndy Pass → Kok-Kiya Valley

    Into the remote highlands

    Off-roadHike

    The morning starts with a visit to Naryn's local bazaar, where you help choose fresh bread, fruit, and vegetables for today's picnic. Then the road heads south into some of the most remote country in Kyrgyzstan. In Bosogo gorge, you stretch your legs on a 1.5-hour hike into the mountains, followed by a picnic lunch in the gorge. In the afternoon, the drive continues over Kyndy Pass, where passports and border permits are checked, and into the wide, quiet Kok-Kiya Valley. You arrive at a small mountain camp, your base for the next two nights, with time to settle in and warm up in a traditional Kyrgyz banya before dinner.

    • DrivingAbout 4–5 hours, partly off-road, with stops
    • HikingAbout 1.5 hours, if the weather allows
    • Highest pointKyndy Pass
    • MealsBreakfast, picnic lunch, dinner
    • OvernightSimple wooden cottage at a mountain camp, Kok-Kiya Valley
    • ShowerNo shower, but a traditional banya (sauna) is included
    • ToiletSimple outdoor toilet, shared
    • Wi-FiWeak Wi-Fi, no phone signal

    Good to know

    This area is a border zone, so please keep your passport with you, not in your main luggage. The off-road sections are bumpy; if you get car sick, let your guide know so you can sit in front. If the weather is bad, the hike is replaced with a short walk in the gorge.

  7. Day7

    Kok-Kiya → Kel-Suu Lake → Kok-Kiya

    Kel-Suu Lake

    RideBoat3,500 m

    Today you discover one of Kyrgyzstan's most hidden places. After breakfast, you ride on horseback with local wranglers for about two hours through the valley to Kel-Suu, a turquoise lake stretching between sheer canyon walls. At the shore, your guide sets up a coffee break by the water, with a glass of champagne to celebrate reaching this remote spot. Then a boat takes you deep into the lake, and if the weather allows, you land for a short hike to a cave above the water. Lunch is a picnic by the lake or a warm meal back at camp, depending on the weather and your choice. The afternoon is free to rest, read, or relax in the banya.

    • Horse ridingAbout 2 hours each way
    • BoatAbout 40 minutes, life jackets provided
    • AltitudeAround 3,500 m at the lake
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch (picnic or at camp), dinner
    • OvernightSimple wooden cottage at a mountain camp, Kok-Kiya Valley
    • ShowerNo shower, but a traditional banya (sauna) is included
    • ToiletSimple outdoor toilet, shared
    • Wi-FiWeak Wi-Fi, no phone signal

    Good to know

    It's cold and windy at the lake, even in summer, so bring a warm jacket, hat, and gloves. The cave hike needs shoes with good grip. In bad weather, the boat trip stays on the water without landing, and lunch moves to the camp.

  8. Day8

    Kok-Kiya → Naryn → Tash-Bashat

    Back to the valleys

    DriveGuesthouse

    After a last morning in the quiet of Kok-Kiya, you drive back over Kyndy Pass toward Naryn, with stops for photos along the way. Lunch is at a restaurant in Naryn, a welcome change after the mountains. In the afternoon, a short drive takes you to the peaceful village of Tash-Bashat, where a family guesthouse welcomes you with a hot shower and time to rest. The rest of the afternoon is free to relax or take an easy walk through the village, before a home-cooked dinner with your hosts.

    • DrivingAbout 5 hours in total, partly off-road, with stops
    • Highest pointKyndy Pass
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, dinner
    • OvernightFamily guesthouse, Tash-Bashat
    • ShowerYes, private bathroom in your room
    • ToiletYes, in your room
    • Wi-FiYes

    Good to know

    Passports are checked again on the way out of the border zone, so keep yours with you.

  9. Day9

    Tash-Bashat → Kochkor → Kyzyl-Tuu → Bokonbaevo

    A day of nomad culture

    Eagle hunterCulture

    Today is dedicated to the crafts and traditions of the nomads. In Kochkor, women from a local workshop show you how shyrdak, the colorful Kyrgyz felt carpets, are made by hand, a craft passed down through generations. After lunch in Kochkor, you drive to the southern shore of Issyk-Kul and the village of Kyzyl-Tuu, famous for its yurt makers. Here a local family shows you how a yurt is built, and you can help put one together. In the late afternoon, you meet a berkutchi, a golden eagle hunter, and watch his eagle fly in a hunting demonstration. The evening continues at his home with a folk music performance and a farewell-style dinner with his family.

    • DrivingAbout 5 hours in total, with stops
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, dinner with the eagle hunter's family
    • OvernightElestet, a modern yurt stay, Bokonbaevo
    • ShowerShared
    • ToiletShared
    • Wi-FiYes

    Good to know

    This is the busiest day of the trip, with three cultural visits. Please ask before taking close-up photos of the eagle, and follow the hunter's instructions around the bird.

  10. Day10

    Bokonbaevo → Balykchy → Kok-Moinok → Bishkek

    Farewell to the mountains

    Issyk-KulCanyon walk

    Your last morning is slow and peaceful. Take time to walk along the shore of Issyk-Kul, swim if the weather is warm, or simply sit by the water. Around midday, you drive along the lake to Balykchy for lunch. In the afternoon, you stop at Kok-Moinok, a hidden landscape of red and orange canyons, for an easy walk of about 50 minutes among the rock formations. Then the road leads back to Bishkek, where you check in to your hotel and celebrate the end of the journey with a farewell dinner at a restaurant in the city.

    • DrivingAbout 5.5 hours in total, with stops
    • WalkingAbout 50 minutes, easy
    • MealsBreakfast, lunch, farewell dinner at a restaurant
    • OvernightDUO hotel, Bishkek, private bathroom
    • ShowerYes, in your room
    • Wi-FiYes

    Good to know

    You arrive in Bishkek at around 19:00. If you have an evening flight on this day, please tell us when you book. We'll skip Kok-Moinok and get you to Bishkek earlier, at around 17:30.
